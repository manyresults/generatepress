The NNN Commercial Lease Scenario

Where the Potential Cap Rate Comes In

A triple-net, or NNN, commercial lease generally allocates specified property expenses to the tenant in addition to base rent.

Depending on the lease, those expenses may include property taxes, insurance, and maintenance.

For an investor, this structure may improve the property's operating economics compared with a lease in which the landlord absorbs those costs.

However, the specific allocation of expenses depends on the executed lease. An NNN arrangement does not automatically eliminate every expense for the property owner.

The projected ~15%+ cap rate is a potential investment scenario, not a current in-place cap rate.

The property is vacant and does not currently have an executed commercial lease establishing the projected rent or expense allocation.

What Does That Mean for the Cap Rate?

The potential cap-rate scenario assumes the property is leased at the projected rents and operated under a lease structure that supports the anticipated net operating income.

A cap rate is calculated using net operating income (NOI), not gross rental income.

The formula is:

Cap Rate = Annual Net Operating Income ÷ Purchase Price × 100

The final cap rate will depend on actual rental income, operating expenses, lease terms, and the purchase price.

Buyers should independently verify the assumptions and calculate their own projected NOI and cap rate before making an investment decision.
