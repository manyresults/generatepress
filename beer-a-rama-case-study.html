Case Study · Retail & Beverage

Beer-A-Rama Utility & Payment Processing Audit

Contacts: Ron Marvo (SPARQ Performance) · Brie LaRosa (Beer-A-Rama)

The Challenge

Beer-A-Rama was paying a high effective rate on its credit card processing, but ownership had no clear way to evaluate whether the fees were competitive. Prior conversations with processors had been murky about what actually counted as a qualifying "savings" under audit terms, making it hard to trust any proposed switch.

On top of that, the store was hesitant to sign a long-term contract without understanding exactly what they'd be locked into and why. Beer-A-Rama needed:

  • A transparent, apples-to-apples audit of their current payment processing costs
  • Clear definitions of what counts as "savings" so results couldn't be inflated or misrepresented
  • Contract terms that only bind the business if real savings are actually delivered

The Solution

SPARQ Performance conducted a full audit of Beer-A-Rama's payment processing and presented three distinct pricing scenarios so the client could compare real trade-offs instead of a single take-it-or-leave-it offer:

  1. Dual Pricing — a 3% surcharge passed to all card transactions.
  2. Traditional Pricing — a lower effective processing rate with no surcharge structure.
  3. Surcharge-Only Pricing — a reduced debit rate paired with a surcharge model.

To address Beer-A-Rama's concerns about vague savings claims, SPARQ clarified in writing that:

  • "Savings" excludes any reduction that comes simply from normal market rate drops — it only counts improvements SPARQ's changes actually deliver.
  • The 36-month contract term only takes effect if the proposed future savings are implemented, protecting the client from being locked in without seeing results.

The Results

The audit uncovered meaningful savings across all three scenarios, giving Beer-A-Rama the flexibility to choose the option that best fit their risk tolerance and customer experience preferences.

Even the most conservative option delivered over $13,000 in annual savings, while the top-performing dual pricing model projected nearly $80,000 per year back into the business — with contract protections in place to ensure Beer-A-Rama only pays for savings actually realized.
