You're Not Alone

Bowel Concerns Are Common — and Treatable

Many women quietly struggle with constipation or bowel leakage, often after childbirth or menopause. These conditions are frequently underreported because they feel embarrassing — but they are medical problems with real, effective solutions. Recognizing the signs is the first step toward relief.

Signs of Constipation

Frequent straining, hard stools, a feeling of incomplete emptying, or two or fewer bowel movements per week.

Signs of Bowel Leakage

Leaking stool while passing gas, urgency you can't reach the bathroom in time for, or a complete loss of bowel control.

How It Affects Daily Life

Planning outings around bathroom access, avoiding exercise or social events, discomfort, and anxiety that quietly narrows your world.

Constipation

What Causes Constipation?

Constipation usually results from several factors working together. Common contributors include a low-fiber diet, inadequate fluid intake, and a sedentary lifestyle. Pregnancy, childbirth, and the hormonal shifts of menopause can weaken the pelvic floor muscles that support normal bowel movements. Certain medications, thyroid or metabolic conditions, irritable bowel syndrome, and pelvic floor dysfunction — where the muscles fail to relax and coordinate properly — can all play a role as well.

Fecal Incontinence

What Is Fecal Incontinence?

Fecal incontinence is the loss of control over bowel movements, causing stool to leak unexpectedly. It ranges from occasional leakage while passing gas to a complete loss of control — and while it can feel isolating, it is very treatable.

Talk to a Specialist

Common Causes

Usually a weakened or damaged pelvic floor and sphincter — most often from childbirth, chronic constipation or diarrhea, nerve conditions, or the natural muscle changes of aging and menopause.

Treatment Options

Most women improve with conservative care — diet changes, bowel training, pelvic floor therapy, and biofeedback. For more advanced cases, sacral neuromodulation or sphincter repair can restore control.

Common Questions

Frequently Asked Questions

Answers to the questions women ask us most — about constipation, bowel leakage, and what getting care actually looks like.

Is bowel dysfunction common in women?

Yes — very. Many women experience constipation or some degree of bowel leakage, especially after childbirth or menopause. It is frequently underreported because it feels embarrassing, but it is a genuine medical condition with effective, proven treatments.

When should I see a specialist about constipation?

If constipation persists despite improving your diet, fluids, and activity — or if you notice pain, bleeding, or a sudden change in your bowel habits — it is time to be evaluated. Persistent constipation can point to an underlying pelvic floor problem that responds well to targeted care.

Can fecal incontinence be treated without surgery?

In most cases, yes. The majority of women improve significantly with conservative care such as dietary changes, bowel training, pelvic floor physical therapy, and biofeedback. Surgery is reserved for more advanced cases, and we always begin with the least invasive option that fits your situation.

Are these conditions related to my pelvic floor?

Often, yes. The pelvic floor muscles play a central role in normal bowel function. Childbirth, aging, and menopause can weaken or disrupt these muscles, which is why a urogynecologist — a specialist in the pelvic floor — is uniquely equipped to diagnose and treat bowel dysfunction.
