Frequently Asked Questions

Answers to the questions women ask us most — about constipation, bowel leakage, and what getting care actually looks like.

Is bowel dysfunction common in women?

Yes — very. Many women experience constipation or some degree of bowel leakage, especially after childbirth or menopause. It is frequently underreported because it feels embarrassing, but it is a genuine medical condition with effective, proven treatments.

When should I see a specialist about constipation?

If constipation persists despite improving your diet, fluids, and activity — or if you notice pain, bleeding, or a sudden change in your bowel habits — it is time to be evaluated. Persistent constipation can point to an underlying pelvic floor problem that responds well to targeted care.

Can fecal incontinence be treated without surgery?

In most cases, yes. The majority of women improve significantly with conservative care such as dietary changes, bowel training, pelvic floor physical therapy, and biofeedback. Surgery is reserved for more advanced cases, and we always begin with the least invasive option that fits your situation.

Are these conditions related to my pelvic floor?

Often, yes. The pelvic floor muscles play a central role in normal bowel function. Childbirth, aging, and menopause can weaken or disrupt these muscles, which is why a urogynecologist — a specialist in the pelvic floor — is uniquely equipped to diagnose and treat bowel dysfunction.

What happens at my first appointment?

Your first visit is a compassionate, confidential conversation. Dr. Littman will listen to your concerns, review your history, and perform a focused evaluation to understand the cause of your symptoms. Together you will build a personalized plan and clear next steps — no pressure, just clarity.
